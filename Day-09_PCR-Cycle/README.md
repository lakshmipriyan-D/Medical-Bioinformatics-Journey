# Day 09 – Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)

## What is PCR?

PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) is a laboratory technique used to make **millions of copies of a specific DNA segment** in a short time.

### Real-Life Example

A doctor receives a patient's blood sample with very little DNA. Instead of testing that tiny amount directly, PCR first copies the target DNA millions of times so it becomes easier to detect diseases like infections or genetic disorders.

---

# PCR Cycle (3 Main Steps)

Each PCR cycle has three important stages.

## 1. Denaturation (95°C)

<AsyncImage query="PCR denaturation DNA double helix separating" aspectRatio="16:9"/>

**Temperature:** Around **95°C**

### What happens?

* The double-stranded DNA is heated.
* Hydrogen bonds break.
* DNA separates into **two single strands**.

Think of it like opening a zipper.

**Purpose:** To expose each DNA strand for copying.

---

## 2. Annealing (50–65°C)

<AsyncImage query="PCR annealing primers binding to DNA strands" aspectRatio="16:9"/>

**Temperature:** Around **50–65°C**

### What happens?

* The reaction cools down.
* Short DNA primers attach to their complementary DNA sequences.

Think of primers as bookmarks showing DNA polymerase where to start.

**Purpose:** Primers prepare the DNA for copying.

---

## 3. Extension (72°C)

<AsyncImage query="PCR extension DNA polymerase extending DNA strands" aspectRatio="16:9"/>

**Temperature:** **72°C**

### What happens?

* Taq DNA Polymerase starts working.
* It adds nucleotides (A, T, G, C).
* A new complementary DNA strand is built.

Think of a worker extending a road from the starting point marked by the primer.

**Purpose:** To create a complete copy of the DNA strand.

---

# PCR Cycle Summary

| **Step**     | **Temperature** | **Main Function**                   |
| ------------ | --------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Denaturation | 95°C            | DNA strands separate                |
| Annealing    | 50–65°C         | Primers attach to complementary DNA |
| Extension    | 72°C            | Taq DNA Polymerase builds new DNA   |

---

# Why is PCR Important?

PCR is widely used for:

* Disease diagnosis
* Genetic testing
* Forensic DNA analysis
* Research laboratories
* Detecting viruses and bacteria

# Key Takeaway

One PCR cycle doubles the target DNA. Repeating the cycle many times produces millions of DNA copies from a very small sample.
