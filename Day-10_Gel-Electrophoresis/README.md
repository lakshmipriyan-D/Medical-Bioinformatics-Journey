# Day 11 – Gel Electrophoresis

## What is Gel Electrophoresis?

Gel Electrophoresis is a laboratory technique used to **separate DNA, RNA, or proteins based on their size and electrical charge**.

It is commonly used after PCR to check whether DNA amplification was successful.

### Real-Life Example

After performing PCR, a scientist loads the DNA sample into a gel. When electricity is applied, the DNA fragments move through the gel, creating visible bands that help identify the DNA fragments.

---

# Principle of Gel Electrophoresis

- DNA has a **negative charge** because of its phosphate backbone.
- When electricity is applied, DNA moves toward the **positive electrode (anode)**.
- Smaller DNA fragments move faster.
- Larger DNA fragments move slower.

---

# Main Components

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| Agarose Gel | Separates DNA fragments |
| Buffer | Conducts electricity |
| DNA Sample | Contains DNA fragments |
| Loading Dye | Helps load and track samples |
| Power Supply | Creates an electric field |

---

# Step-by-Step Process

## Step 1 – Prepare the Gel

- Agarose is mixed with buffer.
- The gel is poured into a mold.
- A comb creates small wells.

## Step 2 – Load the Samples

- DNA is mixed with loading dye.
- Samples are placed into the wells.

## Step 3 – Apply Electricity

- The negative electrode is placed near the wells.
- DNA moves toward the positive electrode.
- Small fragments travel farther than large fragments.

## Step 4 – Visualize the DNA

- The gel is stained.
- UV or blue light reveals DNA bands.

---

# DNA Movement

| Fragment Size | Movement |
|---------------|----------|
| Small | Moves faster |
| Medium | Moderate movement |
| Large | Moves slower |

---

# Key Takeaway

Gel Electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size. Since DNA is negatively charged, it moves toward the positive electrode, and smaller fragments travel farther through the agarose gel.
