# Day 11 – DNA Fingerprinting

## What is DNA Fingerprinting?

DNA Fingerprinting is a technique used to identify an individual by analyzing unique DNA patterns. Every person's DNA pattern is unique (except identical twins), making it useful for identification.

### Real-Life Story

A crime scene contains a small blood sample. Scientists first **amplify** the DNA using **PCR**, then **separate** the DNA fragments using **Gel Electrophoresis**. Finally, they compare the DNA band pattern with a suspect's sample. If the patterns match, it indicates that the DNA samples likely came from the same person.

---

# Step-by-Step Process

## Step 1 – Collect the DNA Sample

* Blood
* Saliva
* Hair root
* Skin cells

## Step 2 – PCR Amplification

* A small amount of DNA is copied into millions of copies.
* This provides enough DNA for analysis.

## Step 3 – Gel Electrophoresis

* DNA fragments move through agarose gel.
* Smaller fragments travel farther.
* Larger fragments travel shorter distances.
* Visible DNA bands are produced.

## Step 4 – Compare the DNA Pattern

Scientists compare the band pattern of:

* Crime scene sample
* Suspect sample

Matching patterns indicate a strong genetic match.

---

# PCR vs Gel Electrophoresis

| PCR                     | Gel Electrophoresis       |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Copies DNA              | Separates DNA             |
| Increases DNA quantity  | Creates visible DNA bands |
| Happens before analysis | Happens after PCR         |

---

# Key Takeaway

DNA Fingerprinting combines **PCR** and **Gel Electrophoresis** to create unique DNA band patterns that help identify individuals in forensic investigations and genetic analysis.
